01

HUMAN DIGNITY

Human Dignity

How do you define human dignity? It is the state or quality of being worthy of honor or respect; every person has an unearned, inner worth and a right to respect something we simply have because we are human, something that should never be violated or taken away, and something that can be described as sacred, where other people must respect it and cannot rightfully abuse Everyone has human dignity that includes you, me, and everyone else. However, not everyone acknowledges the human dignity people have; not everyone realizes human dignity is inherent, inviolable, and inalienable. As seen in our literary work; human dignity is shown through Amethyst’s personal experience of being dismissed simply because of her age. Her parents repeatedly tell her that she “is still a child” therefore she doesn’t know enough about politics to have a valid stance of her own. However, in the eyes of Amethyst’s parents — it is as if they are protecting her, but for Amethyst, that is not the case. What may come off as protection for them appeared to Amethyst as being prevented from being heard and having her own opinion, regardless of her age. With this, Amethyst demonstrates that one’s age should not be the sole basis when determining whether an opinion is worth listening to. She takes the initiative to research the background of both candidates, and share what she learned with her parents. Even if they refused to listen to her, she still continues to share factual information with others. The story therefore reminds us that we should not belittle or disregard others simply because of their age, status, or differences, and that respecting human dignity means recognizing that every person should be treated with respect and deserves to have their own thoughts, feelings, and opinions regarded with respect. Every person possesses an inherent dignity because they are created in the image and likeness of God. Therefore, when we respect others, especially those whose voices are often ignored, we are also recognizing the dignity that God has given them.

CONSCIENCE

Conscience

Conscience serves as our inner voice that allows us to recognize what is right and wrong and ultimately influences the decisions we make in our everyday lives. It allows us to reflect on our actions and consider whether what we are doing is inherently and morally good or harmful. However, it is important to recognize that our conscience does not automatically guarantee that every decision we make is right — which is why it needs to be formed through truth, reflection, proper knowledge, and guidance. Conscience is shown through the different decisions made by the characters in Dekada ‘70 as they experience the injustices and struggles brought by Martial Law. Let’s take Jules for example, he chose to stand up for what he believes is morally right and becomes involved in the battle for freedom despite the risks that accompany his actions. His decision proves that following our conscience does not exactly mean choosing what is easiest or safest, but choosing what we believe is right even when doing so comes with consequences. Similarly, Amanda also develops her conscience throughout the story as she becomes more aware of the problems surrounding her family and the society she lives in. At first, she accepts the traditional role expected of her, but as she witnesses the experiences of her own children and the injustices taking place around them, she begins to question what she once accepted and develops her own understanding of what is right. Through this, Dekada ‘70 reminds us that having a conscience means being willing to recognize injustice and reflect on how we should respond to it. As Christians, we are called to properly form our conscience and allow God’s teachings to guide our decisions, especially when faced with difficult decisions. In doing so, we become more capable of making responsible choices, standing up for what is right, and becoming better individuals.

FREEDOM & RESPONSIBILITY

Freedom & Responsibility

Every day, we are given the choice to choose between one decision or another—to choose between this or that, something that is possible because God has given everyone freedom, which is the power, rooted in reason and will, to act or not to act, and to perform deliberate actions on one's responsibility, whenever freedom is present, it forces us to grow and mature in truth and goodness. A common misinterpretation I often hear is that freedom is simply “doing whatever we want,” which is false in a sense because in reality, freedom is broken into two concepts: authentic and illusory freedom. Authentic freedom is being free to choose what is good and right, even if something else is an easier or tempting choice; it allows us to grow as a person and as a child of God. Meanwhile, illusory freedom is when someone thinks they're free simply because they can do whatever they want; they abuse their freedom and use it to act without consideration of others. Of course, everyone has freedom, but with great freedom comes great responsibility; with every decision we make, responsibility and consequences follow; the action we take affects us, others, and our relationship with God. For example, a friend of yours wears a dress she recently bought, and when she asks you if it looks pretty on her, as a reply you tell her she looks like a pig wearing it and just dismiss it as a joke. In this situation, this is not a good example of pairing our freedom with responsibility because instead of using our freedom to do what is good, we have used it to hurt another person. Our words may leave us feeling guilty after looking back, especially when we realize that we cannot take back what we have said, we may also damage our relationship with our friend by making them feel or hurt, and above all, we weaken our relationship with God because we have chosen to use the freedom to do irresponsible things that goes against His teachings and the way He wants us to treat others.

FORGIVENESS

Forgiveness

Forgiveness is the willingness to let go of anger, resentment, and the desire to hold someone’s mistakes against them, especially when they have caused us pain. It does not mean that we should ignore wrongdoing, pretend that nothing happened, or immediately trust someone who has hurt us. Instead, forgiveness means choosing not to allow anger and resentment to control us while recognizing that people are capable of making mistakes and changing for the better. As seen in our literary work, forgiveness is shown through the characters’ experiences with conflict, misunderstandings, and painful situations. Their experiences remind us that relationships can be affected by the mistakes and decisions of others, but these conflicts do not always have to result in permanent hatred or broken relationships. When a person chooses to understand another’s situation, accept a sincere apology, or let go of resentment, it creates an opportunity for healing and reconciliation. This connects to Christian teachings because Jesus teaches us to forgive others as God forgives our sins. Forgiveness therefore becomes more than simply moving on from an argument; it becomes an act of compassion, humility, and love toward another person. As adolescents, we can apply this teaching when we experience misunderstandings with our friends, classmates, or family members. Instead of immediately responding with anger, spreading the situation to others, or holding a grudge, we can communicate honestly, listen to the other person’s side, acknowledge our own mistakes, and accept sincere apologies. At the same time, forgiveness does not require us to tolerate repeated harmful behavior or abandon healthy boundaries. We can forgive someone while still recognizing that their actions were wrong and allowing them the opportunity to change. Ultimately, the literary work reminds us that forgiveness requires strength because it asks us to choose peace and healing instead of revenge, and to recognize that both giving and receiving forgiveness can help us grow into more understanding and compassionate individuals.

COMPASSION

Compassion

Compassion is the ability to recognize and understand the struggles of others and respond with genuine concern, kindness, and above all: the willingness to help. It goes beyond simply just feeling sorry for someone, because it requires us to notice what another person may be experiencing and choose to respond in a way that we believe can help them. As seen in our literary work, compassion is shown through the different struggles and difficulties experienced by the characters. When a character faces hardship, unfair treatment, or a difficult situation, another person may choose to listen, offer support, or stand beside them instead of judging or ignoring them. These actions demonstrate that compassion allows people to feel understood and reminds them that they never have to face their struggles alone. This reflects the Christian teaching of loving our neighbors and treating everyone with kindness, dignity, and respect. Compassion also requires us to look beyond simply our own experiences because we may not always know what another person is going through. What may be the case for you may not be the case for other people — which is why compassion is vital in making another person feel valued and respected. As Ignacian Marian learners, we can practice compassion in simple ways: such as helping a classmate who is struggling with schoolwork, comforting a friend who is having a difficult day, checking up on someone who seems to be struggling, or including someone who feels left out. Even the smallest acts of kindness can have a meaningful impact because they can make another person feel valued, supported, and remembered. However, compassion does not mean that we have to solve every problem someone faces; sometimes, simply listening and making someone feel heard instead of judged can make a difference. Through compassion, we are able to put Christian values into action by showing God’s love not only through what we say, but also how we treat and care for the people around us.

MORAL TRANSFORMATION

Moral Transformation

Moral transformation is the process of becoming a better person by learning from our mistakes, experiences, and the decisions we make. As seen in our literary work, moral transformation is shown through Amethyst’s growth as she becomes more confident and responsible in expressing her thoughts. Although her parents refuse to listen to her and she becomes frustrated, she chooses to research the candidates and their backgrounds, pray to God for guidance, and responsibly share what she learns with others online. Through this experience, she learns that having a voice also means knowing how to use it properly and responsibly. We can also experience moral transformation through simple situations in our everyday lives. For example, when my parents would ask me to wash the dishes or vacuum the floor, I would usually say “Wait lang” because I was busy using my phone, but I eventually realized that I needed to stop delaying my responsibilities and learn to do them right away. This helped me become more disciplined and responsible. As students, we can practice moral transformation by learning from our mistakes, thinking before we act, taking responsibility for our actions, and asking God for guidance because through these efforts, we improve ourselves, grow closer to God, and become better individuals.

FAITHFUL CHRISTIAN LIVING

Faithful Christian Living

As students, faith helps us in facing hardships in our lives and showing it through our actions. In the story “When the Young Speaks”, you can see that Amethyst shows her deep love for God by praying to ask for guidance when she felt confused and unheard by her parents. After learning the definition, importance, and application of human dignity, conscience, freedom and responsibility, forgiveness, compassion, and moral transformation, we can all connect it to faithful Christian living, which tells us that faith isn't just something about believing but something we practice because believing in God isn't shown only in words but in actions—it refers to trying to live our everyday life according to God’s teachings that teaches us how we strengthen our relationship with God, the things we can do to improve ourself and our lifestyle, how we should treat others, and what should be the basis of our decision making. This allows us to grow closer to God by helping us grow closer to him through prayer and by following his teachings, guides us to become better individuals and to live a better lifestyle by encouraging us to develop good values and habits and incorporate them into our day-to-day lives, help us build a better relationship with others by teaching us to recognize the human dignity of every person, and by influencing our decisions based on God’s teachings and values instead of basing it solely on what we want.

A FINAL REFLECTION

FAITH & LIFE

What we believe shapes how we live.

The different Christian teachings discussed in this literary work remind us that our actions, choices, and relationships with others are deeply connected to our values as individuals and as Christians. Through human dignity, conscience, freedom and responsibility, forgiveness, compassion, and moral transformation, we learn the importance of respecting others, recognizing what is right, taking responsibility for our choices, showing kindness, and continuously improving ourselves. These teachings can be seen not only in the experiences of the characters but also in the situations we encounter in our own lives.

As Ignacian Marian learners, we may still be learning how to make responsible decisions and understand the consequences of our actions, but these teachings remind us that we already have the ability to choose what is good. By listening to our conscience, respecting the dignity of others, practicing compassion and forgiveness, and learning from our mistakes, we can gradually become better individuals. Most importantly, we are reminded that living out our faith is not only about knowing Christian teachings, but also about putting them into practice through the way we think, decide, speak, and treat others every day.
